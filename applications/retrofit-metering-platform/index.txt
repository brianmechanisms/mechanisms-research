3:T819,The moonshot version of the fuel segment. Flow: driver pulls up, pays/authorises in the app, a robot opens the fuel door/cap, inserts the nozzle, dispenses the paid amount, and withdraws. Plays to our robotics/mechanisms strength (grippers, gantries, sensing).

RESEARCH VERDICT (June 2026): this is FAR-FUTURE for the general car market — and as a CORE product line for Kenya/EA it is largely a DISTRACTION. It is not a missing invention (the first public refuelling robot, TankPitstop/Rotec, opened in 2008; Fuelmatics+Husky, Autofuel and a Neste pilot followed) — it has simply never sustained commercial passenger operation in 15+ years. The only place robotic refuelling actually runs commercially is closed MINING fleets (Scott Robofuel, Rotec) — standardized vehicles, private site, costly downtime.

WHY IT STALLS (all outside our control):
· Hazardous-area robotics — every in-zone actuator/camera must be ATEX/IECEx Ex-certified; this is the cost killer. Plus bonding/anti-static, Stage II vapour recovery, fire/spill interlocks.
· Car variability — differing fuel-door releases, cap types and filler locations. No standard filler interface = fundamental blocker for a universal robot.
· Unattended-dispensing law (NFPA 30A / IFC; EPRA-equivalent) often needs an attendant or the user OUTSIDE the car watching the nozzle — directly against the "relax in your car" pitch.
· Economics — the cheapest "robot" is a human, and the EV transition is closing the liquid-fuel window.

PRAGMATIC REFRAME (the useful takeaway): if pursued at all, the transferable value is in (a) outdoor robotic perception/positioning and (b) safe robot-to-vehicle DOCKING — which map onto the more credible adjacent market, ROBOTIC EV CHARGING (exactly where Autofuel itself pivoted). Frame this branch around docking/perception generality and EV charging, not around beating the petrol attendant. Simplest viable petrol version would be capless-filler cars + driver positions the car + a single bolt-on actuator.
