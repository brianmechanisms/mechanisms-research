Design Notes

1. Overview

This mechanism uses a compound epicyclic gear train rolling around a racetrack-shaped closed track to produce quick-return linear motion. The goal is a 1-DOF mechanism where a gripper moves at constant horizontal velocity during the working stroke (stance) and returns quickly during the return stroke (swing), suitable for continuous pick-and-place or linear actuation.

2. Gear Train & Constraints

A compound gear with coaxial pair (r1, r2) meshes with driven gears (r3, r4) on the outer and inner racks respectively. The speed ratio k = r1/r2 determines the quick-return ratio.

Simultaneity constraint:

r1·r4=r2·r3

This ensures both gear pairs complete their semicircular turns together. The track radii are:

R1=r1+2r3 R2=r2+2r4

4. Point P — Velocity-Continuous Transition

Point P sits on the compound gear's radial arm. During stance it is at radius R2, during swing at R1. During turns, P's radius transitions via sinusoidal interpolation:

dP(θ) = R1+R22 − R1−R22 ·sin(θ)

where θ sweeps from π/2 to −π/2 during each turn.

5. Point Q — Constant vx Hold Zone

Point Q extends the constant-velocity region into the turns by an angle φ. During the hold zone, Q must maintain vx = r2 while the compound gear rotates around the semicircular arc.

With the compound center at the turn center and the radial arm at angle θ, the full horizontal velocity of a point at varying radius d is:

vx= ωC· [d·sin(θ) − d′·cos(θ)]

Setting vx = R2 (since ωC = 1) gives the ODE:

d·sin(θ) − d′·cos(θ) =R2

Which can be rewritten as:

ddθ [d·cos(θ)] =−R2

Integrating with the boundary condition d(π/2) = R2:

d(θ)= R2·(π2−θ)cos(θ)

At θ = π/2 this evaluates via L'Hôpital's rule to d = R2, confirming continuity.

6. Cubic Hermite Transition

At the boundary between the hold zone (angle π/2 − φ) and the transition zone, both the radius and its derivative must be continuous. The hold zone ends with:

dend= R2·φsin(φ) dend′= R2·(φcosφ−sinφ)sin2(φ)

A cubic Hermite polynomial interpolates from (dend, d′end) to (R1, 0) over the remaining angular span π − φ, ensuring C1 continuity of the velocity profile.

7. Output Configurations

7a. Bell Crank Configuration

The racetrack frame is the fixed body. Point Q on the compound gear is the output, connected via a rigid bell-crank arm through a slider on a horizontal rail to a gripper. The slider’s horizontal position tracks Q’s x-coordinate.

This works well for a single mechanism set. However, for continuous motion at least two sets are required — one gripping during stance while the other returns during swing — so that at least one set is always in the working stroke.

Problem: With two sets in bell-crank configuration, the two compound gears occupy the same racetrack and must be 180° out of phase. Connecting them rigidly so they can be driven by a single actuator is geometrically very difficult — their centers are at different positions on the track and the relative geometry between them changes throughout the cycle.

7b. Slotted Slider Configuration

The slider is the body (ground). The gripper is mounted on the racetrack rack section, which now moves relative to the slider body. Q drives the slider through a vertical slot, translating Q’s horizontal motion directly to the body.

Advantage: Since the body (slider) is common to both sets, each compound gear sees the same relative motion with respect to the body regardless of phase. The two compound gears of the two sets can therefore be connected by rigid links and driven by a single actuator — they maintain fixed relative positions because their motion relative to the moving body is identical.

This makes the slotted slider the preferred configuration for continuous-motion applications requiring multiple phase-offset mechanism sets.
