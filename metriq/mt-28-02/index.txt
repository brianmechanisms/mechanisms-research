3:Tc8c,## Intent

A rotary check valve built from **five nested curved shells** — wedges —
that all share **one hinge** at the centre. Bottom to top they run blue,
green, yellow, orange, red; blue is the biggest and outermost, red the
smallest and innermost. Each shell's outer radius steps down by one wall
thickness, so every shell fits *inside* the one below it, like a fan or a
telescope folded round a pivot.

## Fan out to seal, nest to clear

The opening parameter is **theta**, measured anticlockwise from red's fixed
edge at **90°**:

- **theta = 0 — OPEN.** All five shells are stacked on the same slot, collapsed
  onto the fixed red core. The valve's whole footprint is a single shell wide;
  the bore beside it is clear.
- **theta = 50° — CLOSED.** Blue swings the full angle, the inner shells each
  swing a proportional share, and they fan out to fill the arc. Blue's leading
  face — drawn heavy, as the closing/seal face — reaches the **170° stop** and
  spans the bore.

Red is the **fixed core**: it never moves, and every other shell is measured
from its 90° edge. Blue (outermost) travels the whole theta; each shell inward
travels less, so they nest progressively rather than colliding.

## The stop

170° is a hard geometric limit, not a chosen angle. Blue's leading face sits at
`90 + theta + SEG`; when that reaches 170° the fan is fully open across the
bore and cannot swing further. That is the seal position.

## The return spring

A coil spring runs from **red's fixed top-right corner** to the **midpoint of
blue's face line**. As blue fans out to close, the spring is the element pulling
it there; as force from beneath drives the shells to nest, the spring is
stretched and stores the energy to close again.

## Check-valve actuation

The physical story is a **spring-return check valve**:

- **Force from below pushes the valve OPEN** — the shells telescope together
  and nest (theta → 0), clearing the bore.
- **Releasing the force lets the spring pull it CLOSED** — the shells fan back
  out to the 170° stop and seal.

So flow (or a pusher) from one side opens it; the spring shuts it when that
force drops. It passes one way and holds the other, which is the check-valve
action.

## Prior art

No exact prior art was found. The closest analogues each miss the defining
feature:

- **Iris diaphragm / iris damper.** Overlapping blades open and close an
  aperture, but each blade rides its **own pivot around the perimeter** — the
  blades do not nest, and there is no single shared hinge.
- **Rotary camera shutter.** Coaxial sector blades do rotate about a common
  axis, but they are **timing shutters** — they sweep an opening past a frame,
  not fan rigid shells out to a mechanical stop to seal a bore.
- **Spring-return swing / multi-flapper check valves.** These share the
  open-by-flow, close-by-spring actuation, but their leaves are **flat and
  independent** — they flap, they do not nest concentrically.

The novel element is **rigid concentric shells that rotationally nest about a
single shared hinge**, combined with **check-valve actuation** (open by force
from beneath, close by return spring). None of the analogues combines those.5:["slug","mt-28-02","d"]
